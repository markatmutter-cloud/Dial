## Model context

The Omega Seamaster 300 (ref. 165.024) sits within Omega's professional tool-watch line, introduced in 1957 as a purpose-built diver alongside the Speedmaster (chronograph) and Railmaster (anti-magnetic) — the so-called 'professional trilogy.' The 165.024, produced from roughly 1962/64 through 1969, is the third-generation Seamaster 300, characterised by a 42mm twisted lyre-lug case, fully graduated bakelite bezel, no-date matte black dial, sword handset and tritium lume. It supplanted the straight-lug 2913 (1957) and the transitional 14755 / 165.014 (1960–64). Demand for the watch came from genuine professional use — it was commissioned by the British MoD and issued to Royal Navy divers, Royal Marines and the British Army between 1967 and 1971, with a small but real footprint among other militaries (notably the Israeli Defence Forces). On the civilian side, it competed directly with the Rolex Submariner and Blancpain Fifty Fathoms as a 200m-rated tool diver, but production volumes and length-of-run meant it has historically traded for less than either. The lineage is also the foundation of Omega's modern Seamaster Diver 300M (Pierce Brosnan/Daniel Craig-era Bond watch) introduced in 1993 — a separate, modern line that shares the name and a broad design language but no direct case parts. The 165.024's appeal today is grounded in originality scarcity (heavily affected by WatchCo-era parts assembly), military provenance, and a design considered by many to be Omega's most fully resolved dive watch.

## Agreements & conflicts (2)

- **Sword hands on the 165.024** — The likely reality is a running change — early-production 165.024s carried over batons from the late 165.014, with sword hands becoming standard during the run — but sources do not pin down the exact transition. Be cautious about treating sword hands as universally correct for every 165.024.  ·  _scope: 165.024_
  - Hairspring's 'Big Triangle' write-up describes the 165.024 as introducing the sword handset alongside the lyre-lug case and graduated bezel — i.e. sword hands are the canonical 165.024 handset.
  - Hairspring's fourth 165.024 find notes 'some had sword hands, though this '63 watch does not,' implying a baton-handed early-production variant and/or that not all 165.024s carry sword hands.
- **Case diameter (41 vs 42mm)** — The case is typically described at 42mm including bezel; the 41mm figure likely reflects a different measuring point. Worth flagging rather than asserting a single figure.  ·  _scope: 165.024_
  - Most editorial sources and Hodinkee Shop listings cite the 165.024 case as 42mm.
  - Two Hodinkee Shop listings give the diameter as 41mm with a 13mm thickness.
