## Model context

The Omega Speedmaster was launched in 1957 as a motorsport chronograph — part of a trio of professional Omega tools released that year alongside the Seamaster 300 and Railmaster. Its defining innovation was moving the tachymeter scale from the dial onto the bezel, a first that many brands later copied. Omega initially marketed it as a 'wrist computer' for engineers and racing drivers, calling the bezel a 'Tacho-Productometer.' The watch's trajectory changed in the 1960s when NASA, looking for a chronograph that astronauts could rely on outside the spacecraft, ran a procurement process initiated by engineer James Ragan. Watches from Omega, Rolex, Longines and Hamilton were tested between October 1964 and March 1965; only the Speedmaster survived the full battery of thermal, vacuum, shock, vibration, humidity, oxygen and acoustic tests, and on 1 March 1965 it was certified 'flight-qualified for all manned space missions.' It went on to be worn on every Apollo mission, was on the wrist of Buzz Aldrin when he stepped onto the lunar surface on 20 July 1969, and was used by the Apollo 13 crew to time the 14-second engine burn that corrected their re-entry trajectory. The 105.012 and 145.012 sit at the heart of this story: they are the asymmetric-case, crown-guard, calibre 321 Speedmasters that were on NASA's wrists during the Moon landings, including Armstrong's and Aldrin's own watches on Apollo 11. Demand for the Speedmaster has always rested on this double identity — a genuine 1957 racing instrument that became one of the most documented artefacts of 20th-century exploration — and on the fact that the modern watch remains visually and mechanically close to what flew.

## Marks (8)

- **Asymmetric 'twisted lyre' lugs with crown guards** — Introduced on the 105.012, the case grows outward on the 9 o'clock side to shield the crown and pushers — the first crown-guard Speedmaster case and the silhouette that defines the modern Moonwatch.  
  [1](https://monochrome-watches.com/exclusive-truth-real-armstrong-aldrin-speedmaster-references-how-omega-speedmaster-became-moonwatch/) · [2](https://wristbuddys.com/blogs/blog/the-story-of-the-omega-speedmaster-from-the-racetrack-to-space)  ·  _scope: 105.012, 145.012_
- **Calibre 321** — The column-wheel Lemania-based chronograph movement that powered every NASA-certified Speedmaster through the Moon landings, including the 105.012 and 145.012. Replaced by the cam-actuated 861 in 1968–69 and revived in low-volume form by Omega in 2019.  
  [1](https://monochrome-watches.com/exclusive-truth-real-armstrong-aldrin-speedmaster-references-how-omega-speedmaster-became-moonwatch/) · [2](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/understanding-the-omega-speedmaster-professional) · [3](https://www.gq.com/story/omega-speedmaster-moon-landing)  ·  _scope: 105.012, 145.012_
- **42 mm case, ~13 mm thick, 46 mm lug-to-lug** — Dimensions set in the 105.012 era and effectively unchanged today; widely cited as a reason for the watch's enduring wearability.  
  [1](https://quillandpad.com/2025/03/04/omega-speedmaster-moonwatch-the-making-of-an-icon-2/) · [2](https://monochrome-watches.com/exclusive-truth-real-armstrong-aldrin-speedmaster-references-how-omega-speedmaster-became-moonwatch/)  ·  _scope: 105.012, 145.012, modern_
- **'Flight-Qualified by NASA for all Manned Space Missions — The First Watch Worn on the Moon' caseback** — The engraved caseback text that first appeared after Apollo 11 and has been carried ever since; the modern 3861 generation refines it to 'Flight-Qualified by NASA in 1965…'.  
  [1](https://www.baileybox.com/blog/history-of-omega-speedmaster-moonwatch/) · [2](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/omega-speedmaster-3861-complete-buyers-guide) · [3](https://www.thingap.com/how-a-flight-qualified-watch-helped-save-apollo-13/)  ·  _scope: model-wide, modern_
- **Tachymeter bezel (originally 'Tacho-Productometer')** — The 1957 Speedmaster was the first chronograph to move the tachymeter scale onto the bezel; Omega's own marketing called this scale the 'Tacho-Productometer.'  
  [1](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/understanding-the-omega-speedmaster-professional) · [2](https://quillandpad.com/2025/03/04/omega-speedmaster-moonwatch-the-making-of-an-icon-2/)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **Hesalite crystal** — Domed acrylic crystal carried over from the NASA era; preferred for spaceflight because it would not shatter dangerously in a vacuum. Omega still offers a Hesalite Moonwatch alongside the sapphire version, complete with an etched Omega logo in the centre of the crystal.  
  [1](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/omega-speedmaster-3861-complete-buyers-guide) · [2](https://www.thingap.com/how-a-flight-qualified-watch-helped-save-apollo-13/)  ·  _scope: model-wide, modern_

## Agreements & conflicts (4)

- **How NASA selected the Speedmaster** — Monochrome, working with the Omega Museum and Ragan directly, explicitly debunks the local-store / three-year-testing version that has been repeated in many earlier articles.  ·  _scope: model-wide_
  - Folk legend: a young NASA engineer bought watches off the shelf in a Houston store and ran three years of tests.
  - Documented account: James Ragan ran an official RFQ process; testing lasted about four months (Oct 1964 – Mar 1965) and four brands (Omega, Rolex, Longines, Hamilton) responded.
- **Reference of Aldrin's Apollo 11 Speedmaster** — Monochrome's piece is the primary source for the corrected attribution and notes that the original NASA documentation now supports it.  ·  _scope: 105.012, 145.012_
  - Earlier reporting often cited ST145.012 for Aldrin's watch.
  - Omega Museum / Ragan archive research concludes both Armstrong and Aldrin wore ST105.012; Aldrin's watch was subsequently lost in transit.
- **Number of Apollo missions on which a Speedmaster reached the lunar surface** — There is some looseness in the source corpus about how to count 'Moon landings' — six crewed lunar surface missions occurred (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17), and on Apollo 15 the Speedmaster was at least carried even if not worn outside. Worth flagging rather than smoothing.  ·  _scope: model-wide_
  - Quill & Pad (2025): 'The Omega Speedmaster landed 6 times on the moon.'
  - NY Post and the Dave Scott Apollo 15 Bulova account: on Apollo 15, Scott's Speedmaster crystal popped off and he switched to a Bulova prototype, so a Speedmaster was not on his wrist on the surface that mission.
- **Which Apollo 13 astronaut's Speedmaster was used to time the 14-second burn** — Only Thingap names a specific wrist; other sources stay general. Worth verifying against Omega's own materials before committing to a name.  ·  _scope: model-wide_
  - Thingap: it was Jack Swigert's Speedmaster.
  - Wristbuddys / Quill & Pad / Bailey's: phrased generically as 'the crew's Speedmasters.'

## Stories worth telling (9)

- **Norman M. Morris and the Life magazine cover** — Ed White's Gemini 4 spacewalk made the cover of Life with a Speedmaster visible on his suit. Omega's US distributor Norman M. Morris phoned NASA to ask 'was that our watch up there?' — Omega had no idea its product had been worn on the first American EVA. A reminder that the watch's space career was driven by the astronauts themselves before it was driven by marketing.  
  [source](https://www.gq.com/story/omega-speedmaster-moon-landing)  ·  _scope: early_
- **Armstrong's watch stays behind** — On Apollo 11 the lunar module's electronic timer was misbehaving, so Armstrong left his Speedmaster inside the LM as a backup. That is the reason Aldrin's wrist, not Armstrong's, carried the first watch onto the lunar surface — a small operational decision that produced one of the watch's most quoted lines.  
  [source](https://wristbuddys.com/blogs/blog/the-story-of-the-omega-speedmaster-from-the-racetrack-to-space)  ·  _scope: 105.012_
- **Aldrin: 'a badge of honour'** — Aldrin recalled receiving his Speedmaster while on the backup crew for Gemini 9 and described it as a badge of honour worn through training. He also noted that astronauts didn't actually use GMT in orbit — they ran on Ground Elapsed Time from launch — making the watch as much a personal artefact as a strict navigational tool.  
  [source](https://www.vanityfair.com/news/2009/06/astronaut-buzz-aldrin-lands-at-the-omega-store-in-new-york-city)  ·  _scope: 105.012_
- **Apollo 13's 14-second burn** — With most spacecraft systems shut down to save power, the crew used a Speedmaster to time a 14-second LM-engine burn that corrected their re-entry trajectory. NASA later gave Omega its Silver Snoopy Award.  
  [source](https://www.thingap.com/how-a-flight-qualified-watch-helped-save-apollo-13/)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **Dave Scott's missing crystal and the Bulova on Apollo 15** — The story goes that the crystal of Scott's Speedmaster popped off on the lunar surface, and he switched to a prototype Bulova chronograph for the second EVA — the only non-Omega ever worn on the Moon by a NASA astronaut, later auctioned for $1.5 million.  
  [source](https://nypost.com/2026/04/08/us-news/artemis-ii-astronauts-wore-top-of-the-line-omega-wristwatches-around-the-moon-and-one-slipped-on-an-old-school-nasa-icon/)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **Artemis II keeps the tradition alive** — On the April 2026 Artemis II flight, the four-astronaut crew were issued X-33s as standard, but Victor Glover was photographed in orbit also wearing a mechanical Speedmaster Professional — visibly linking the modern programme back to Apollo.  
  [source](https://nypost.com/2026/04/08/us-news/artemis-ii-astronauts-wore-top-of-the-line-omega-wristwatches-around-the-moon-and-one-slipped-on-an-old-school-nasa-icon/)  ·  _scope: modern_
- **The Speedmaster nearly got cancelled** — Omega CEO Raynald Aeschlimann told GQ that in the 1980s the brand's management committee debated discontinuing the Speedmaster for years before deciding to keep it. The watch that defines Omega today survived an internal vote.  
  [source](https://www.gq.com/story/omega-speedmaster-moon-landing)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **White dials for hot places: the Alaska Project** — Omega's 1969 'Alaska' prototype used a titanium case wrapped in a red anodised aluminium heat-shield and a white dial chosen for thermal reflection. NASA decided not to adopt it; cosmonauts later flew the second-generation Alaska between 1977 and 1981 — and the white dial finally entered the modern catalogue properly in 2024.  
  [source](https://www.wired.com/story/white-omega-speedmaster-moonwatch-2024)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **The 1,014 gold tributes** — In November 1969 Omega marked the Moon landing by producing 1,014 yellow-gold Speedmasters (BA145.022), the first commemorative Speedmaster ever. Nixon was offered one but couldn't keep it due to White House protocol. In 2019 Omega recreated the run in the same number, in a new palladium-bearing 'Moonshine Gold' alloy.  
  [source](https://www.wired.com/story/omega-speedmaster-apollo-11-50th-anniversary)  ·  _scope: gold_
