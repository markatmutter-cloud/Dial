## Model context

The Datejust is Rolex's archetypal date-on-dial watch, introduced in 1945 as the brand's 40th-anniversary flagship and the first wristwatch with an automatically and instantly changing date. Originally produced only in precious metals on the then-new Jubilee bracelet, it eventually expanded to steel (1953, ref. 6305) and two-tone. The 16xx generation (1959–1977) is where the watch settled into its now-iconic form: Cyclops crystal, fluted/smooth/engine-turned bezel options, pie-pan dial, baton hands. References 1601 (fluted white-gold bezel) and 16014 (its five-digit successor with quickset date, sapphire came later with 162xx) anchor this page. The Datejust's appeal is its near-universal wearability, mechanical robustness, and a near-endless variety of dials, bezels, bracelets and materials — which is also the source of the demand: a buyer can specify a Datejust into almost any role from formal to casual.

- **Folded vs. solid Jubilee** — Early Jubilees used hollow, folded links; Rolex transitioned to half-solid (solid outer links) around 1974 within the 16xx era — so this is not a clean handover at the 16xxx changeover as is sometimes claimed.  
  [1](https://www.fratellowatches.com/vintage_datejust_buying_guide/) · [2](https://www.fratellowatches.com/a-closer-look-at-the-sheer-variety-found-in-vintage-rolex-datejust-features/)  ·  _scope: 1601, model-wide_
- **Reeded bezel** — Domed bezel with very fine teeth used on early Datejusts (pre-fluted); broadly used across the industry at the time and replaced on Datejust by the fluted bezel around 1953.  
  [1](https://www.fratellowatches.com/a-closer-look-at-the-sheer-variety-found-in-vintage-rolex-datejust-features/) · [2](https://www.fratellowatches.com/five-rolex-datejust-facts-you-probably-did-not-know/)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **White-gold-lug tell** — Vintage white-gold Datejusts (e.g. 16019) show a small infill on the inside corners of the lugs, mirroring Day-Date midcase construction — a useful field-ID detail since they otherwise look like steel.  
  [1](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/bring-a-loupe-january-17-2025) · [2](https://www.fratellowatches.com/white-gold-rolex-datejust-with-a-burl-wood-dial/)  ·  _scope: model-wide_

## Agreements & conflicts (3)

- **What 'obsidian' 1600 dials actually are** — Even with documented Rolex-to-Royal-Family provenance on one example examined by Ku, the source concludes the truth is unsettled and the 'obsidian' name is likely collector terminology rather than a Rolex designation.  ·  _scope: model-wide_
  - Hairspring (the article's author) and Eric Ku argue the most likely answer is that these are dyed onyx dials that have de-bonded/discoloured over time — they don't look like obsidian to a gemologist, the 'lifting' ring around the handstack recurs too consistently to be natural veining, and a known-onyx Patek dial of similar appearance supports the onyx-degradation theory.
  - An alternative reading is that they are genuinely obsidian dials where the protective varnish has reacted with the adhesive; a third (least likely per the author) is that they are simply fake.
- **Whether lavender 1601 dials are factory-original or aged grey/silver dials** — The catalogue evidence makes a strong case for factory lavender, at least as a Japan-market option, but the topic remains debated in the wider collector community.  ·  _scope: 1601_
  - Hodinkee-style 'tropical / changed-over-time' interpretation: these started life as grey or silver and shifted hue.
  - Wind Vintage / Eric Wind position, supported by a 1972 Rolex Japan catalogue scan, that lavender was a factory dial option — bolstered by no-lume examples whose paint shouldn't have been affected by tritium and which present a consistent, edge-to-edge lavender hue.
- **Authorship of the Oysterquartz design lineage** — Mentioned here because Oysterquartz Datejusts share this lineage; treat the Genta attribution as contested.  ·  _scope: model-wide_
  - James Dowling attributes the predecessor ref. 5100 'Texan' (and by extension the Oysterquartz aesthetic) to Gérald Genta.
  - Brandon Baines's Fratello article on Rolex's 'Nautilina' disputes the Genta attribution.

A vivid illustration of how Datejusts are actually lived with.

- **The 1972 Rolex Japan catalogue and the lavender dial** — A catalogue scan surfaced by Charlie Dunne at Wind Vintage that documents lavender as a factory dial colour — meaningful evidence in a long-running debate about whether lavender 1601 dials are original.

## Module candidates — future nodes (8)

- **Oysterquartz Datejust (ref. 17000/17013/17014)** — Distinct case architecture, caliber 5035, 25-year production run (1977–2002), contested Genta lineage and a growing pre-owned following — deserves its own page rather than crowding the 1601/16014 reference.
- **Datejust Turn-O-Graph** — A spin-off line with a rotating bezel (e.g. 1625, 116263, 116264) that is functionally and visually distinct from the standard Datejust and routinely treated as 'adopted family' rather than core.
- **Datejust II (2009–2016) and Datejust 41 (2016–)** — The 41mm sizing experiment is a separate era with its own movements (3136, later 3235) and a different design intent; warrants its own write-up so the vintage 1601/16014 page can stay focused.
- **Vintage Rolex Datejust dial taxonomy** — Pie-pan vs flat, linen, tapestry, Buckley, Wide Boy, wood (burl/birch), stone (onyx/malachite/tiger eye/lapis), no-lume/Japan, lavender — a standalone illustrated taxonomy would serve buyers far better than scattered references.
- **Spotting a fake or refinished vintage Datejust dial** — Already a self-contained tutorial in the corpus (blank stamping, font, coronet, applique geometry, lume behaviour); pairs naturally with the dial taxonomy as buyer-protection content.
- **Datejust ref. 6305 and the 1953 transitions** — 6305 is the inflection point — first steel Datejust, introduction of the fluted bezel, and the run-up to the Cyclops — a focused page on this reference would anchor the pre-16xx story.
- **Early/bubbleback Datejusts (44xx, 50xx, 6xxx 'Ovettone')** — Pre-1959 references with bubbleback cases, dauphine/leaf hands, coin-edge bezels and gilt OCC dials are mechanically and aesthetically a different watch from the 1601, and currently underserved here.
- **Stone dials on gold Datejusts** — Tiger eye, malachite, lapis, and the 'obsidian/onyx' debate together form a rich subject that would benefit from its own piece — including the construction note (stone bonded to a metal base plate) and the Eric Ku / Loupe This investigation.
