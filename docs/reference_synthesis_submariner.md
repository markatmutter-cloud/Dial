## Model context

The Rolex Submariner sits at the centre of the brand's professional/tool-watch identity. Conceived in the early 1950s under the influence of board member René-Paul Jeanneret — a diving enthusiast close to Jacques Cousteau — it was Rolex's deliberate move into purpose-built sports watches, alongside the Explorer and Turn-O-Graph. The Submariner was launched at the 1954 Basel Fair (Rolex internally dates production to 1953), debuting as the first wristwatch publicly rated to 100m. From the 6204/6205/6200 'pre-crown-guard' era through the Big Crown 6536/6538/5510/5508, Rolex iterated rapidly: bigger crowns, deeper ratings (200m by the 6200), the Mercedes hand, tritium lume (from 5508), and finally crown guards in 1959 with the 5512. The 5512 (1959–1980) and 5513 (1962–1989) define the long crown-guard, no-date, four-digit core: the 5512 is the chronometer-certified watch (calibres 1530/1560/1570), the 5513 the non-chronometer sibling (1530/1520) introduced as a more accessible variant. The 5513's near-three-decade run made it Rolex's everyman dive watch and the reference under which most special-purpose variants were issued (MilSub 5513, COMEX, Explorer-dial, the 'Shadow' South African pieces). The date arrived in 1969 with the 1680, prompted in part by the Sea-Dweller's 1967 introduction; the 1680 was also the first gold Submariner. Demand was driven by post-war recreational diving (Cousteau, the Aqua-Lung), military and commercial-dive contracts (Royal Navy, COMEX), and a steady drumbeat of cultural placement — most famously Sean Connery's 6538 in Dr. No and later Roger Moore's modified 5513 in Live and Let Die. The Submariner is widely regarded as the template the rest of the dive-watch industry followed.

## Agreements & conflicts (4)

- **Who invented the helium release valve** — The patent record is unambiguously Rolex (Zibach), but the engineering priority is contested. Perezcope's 2020 and 2025 articles disagree with each other in tone: the 2020 piece doubts Doxa's claims; the 2025 piece, citing newly surfaced Swiss press, argues Doxa was effectively first to a working valve.
  - Rolex narrative: T. Walker Lloyd brought Bob Barth's idea to Rolex; Rolex filed CH492246 on 6 Nov 1967 (inventor André Zibach), granted 15 Jun 1970, and the Sea-Dweller followed.
  - Doxa narrative: Doxa co-developed the valve, tested it via Claude Wesly and introduced the Sub 300 T Conquistador in 1969 — shared patent claim per Urs Eschle.
  - Perezcope's 2025 evidence: a working Doxa prototype was demonstrably tested during Comex 'Ludion 2' (15 Nov 1967), just nine days after Rolex's filing, suggesting Doxa was at least parallel and arguably ahead in practical execution.
- **Which Submariner came first — 6204 or 6200** — All sources agree the question is unsettled in Rolex's own archives; the 'lower-number-first' theory is presented as plausible folklore, not documented fact.
  - Monochrome and Hodinkee treat the 6204 as the first reference to carry 'Submariner' on the dial (II.53-stamped cases), with the 6200 appearing later despite its lower number.
  - Rolex Passion Market and Rolex Magazine entertain the view that the 6200 was developed first as the Big-Crown 200m Sub but held back by Wilsdorf as too aggressive for retailers, with the slimmer 6204 launched in its place.
- **How rare are Red Subs** — The premium is real, but Fratello pushes back on the assumption that 'Red' equals 'rare' — condition and dial Mk are the more meaningful axes.
  - Common framing: Red 1680s are the prized early variant, with premiums over white-text 1680s (Hodinkee, Fratello, Monochrome).
  - Fratello's vintage round-up: the 1680 was produced for most of its run with red text; total 1680 production was over 110,000, so Red Subs aren't truly rare in absolute terms.
- **5512 vs 5513 as the 'better' watch** — No source ranks these laterally as a hierarchy — the consensus is they are sibling watches with different priorities.
  - Wind Vintage: explicitly refuses to call the 5512 superior, arguing condition trumps reference and that a great 5513 beats a poor 5512.
  - Hodinkee/Fratello: noted preference among some collectors for the 5513's two-line dial and lateral parity with the 5512 on everything but the movement.
  - Monochrome: frames the 5512 as the 'premium' option of the two at the time of sale, owing to its chronometer certification.

## Module candidates — future nodes (10)

- **MilSub (5513 / 5517)** — Distinct MoD specification (sword hands, fixed lugs, circled-T, fully graduated bezel), well-defined production window mid-1970s, and a clear lateral relationship to 'The Shadow' and other military-issue pieces.
- **COMEX Submariner (5513 / 5514 / 1680)** — COMEX partnership from c.1970 produced a discrete family of dials and case modifications (HEVs on 5513/5514, no HEV on 1680), plus its own provenance ecosystem worth a dedicated page.
- **Sea-Dweller (1665 onwards)** — The 1967 Sea-Dweller is the direct trigger for the 1680 date Sub; the Single Red / Double Red lineage and the gas-escape-valve story (including the Doxa controversy) deserve their own treatment.
- **1680 Red Sub** — Mk I–VI (plus service Mk VII) dial taxonomy, meters-first vs feet-first, and the watershed introduction of the date make this a self-contained module.
- **Gold Submariner (1680/8, 16808, 16618, 116618LN, 116619LB 'Smurf')** — VRFM's dedicated gold-Sub article shows the depth of variation (nipple dials, tropical blues, alloy evolution) — distinct enough from the steel core to warrant separation.
- **James Bond Submariner** — 6538 (Connery), 5513 (Moore), and the documented screen-used props at Phillips/Christie's are a cohesive cultural module that otherwise risks dominating the main 5512/5513 narrative.
- **Pre-crown-guard Subs (6204 / 6205 / 6200 / 6536 / 6538 / 5508 / 5510)** — The 1953–1959 era has enough internal variation (Big Crown vs small crown, Explorer dials, OCC vs non-chronometer, radium → tritium transition with the 5508) to merit its own anchor page.
- **5513 matte-dial taxonomy (Meters First → Maxi V)** — 5513mattedial.com alone is a dense reference that doesn't fit neatly inside a model-line overview; ideal as a dedicated identification module.
- **Submariner peer set (Fifty Fathoms, Seamaster 300, Doxa Sub 300, Tudor Submariner, Ploprof)** — Treating these laterally — not as alternatives 'beneath' the Sub — gives readers genre context; Watchfinder and Fratello provide a ready starting list.
- **Five-digit transition (14060 / 16800 / 16610 / 16610LV)** — The bridge from the 5513's late glossy dial to the modern 41mm Sub is a coherent story (sapphire, unidirectional bezel, 904L, ceramic) and is explicitly excluded by Hodinkee's Reference Points.
