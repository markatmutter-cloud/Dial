## Model context

The Rolex Submariner is widely regarded as the archetypal modern dive watch and one of the most culturally pervasive sports watches ever produced. It emerged from the post-war boom in recreational scuba diving — propelled by Cousteau and Gagnan's Aqua-Lung — and from the vision of René-Paul Jeanneret, a Rolex director and keen diver who pushed the brand to build a sporty-yet-elegant waterproof watch. Rolex draws on its 1926 Oyster case and 1931 Perpetual rotor as the technical foundation. The first models appeared in 1953–54, with a 1953 Deep Sea Special strapped to Auguste Piccard's Bathyscaphe descending to 3,131.8m serving as the launch's marketing centrepiece. The 5512 (1959) introduced crown guards and the 40mm case that became the modern blueprint; the 5513 (1962–1989) ran alongside it as the non-chronometer sibling and became the longest-running, most-produced classic Submariner — the watch most people picture when they think 'Submariner'. The model line later branched into the date-equipped 1680, the gold 1680/8, the saturation-diving Sea-Dweller (1665), MilSub/COMEX variants, and the modern 14060/114060/124060 lineage. Demand was driven by Bond, professional diver use (COMEX, SEALAB, Tektite), and the watch's transcendence of its tool-watch origins into a near-default luxury sport watch.

## Agreements & conflicts (4)

- **Who invented the helium-escape valve first** — There is no documentary evidence in the Rolex patent of co-development with Doxa. Urs Eschle's claim of a shared patent appears unsupported. Useful as a 'sea-dweller' / 'comex' context note rather than as a 5512/5513 claim.  ·  _scope: sea-dweller, comex, dive-watch-context_
  - Standard Rolex narrative (Monochrome, Bob's Watches, Rolex's own materials): Rolex invented and patented the HEV, with the patent filed 6 November 1967 (inventor André Zibach) and granted June 1970.
  - Perezcope's 2025 update: Doxa demonstrated a working HEV prototype at Comex's hyperbaric centre during 'Ludion 2' (15–28 November 1967) — just 9 days after Rolex's patent filing — and the Doxa Sub 300 Conquistador was shown publicly in April 1968, suggesting Doxa was at least parallel and possibly ahead in prototyping, though Rolex secured the patent.
- **5512 vs 5513 production ratio** — The corpus's only primary-sourced figure is the Rolex-cited 1:10. The 1:20 figure remains common in market-facing writing.  ·  _scope: 5512, 5513_
  - Rolex's own 2024 publication cites 17,338 (5512) vs 151,449 (5513), i.e. roughly 1:10.
  - Older market lore (cited by Charlie Dunne / Hodinkee) traditionally estimated about 1:20.
- **6204 vs 6200 — which was the first Submariner** — All sources agree the 6200 has a lower number but appeared chronologically after the 6204; the dispute is about which was developed/produced first internally.  ·  _scope: early, model-wide_
  - Most historians (Monochrome, Hodinkee, Bob's Watches) treat the 6204 as the first Submariner (early-1953 / II.53 caseback stamps), with the 6200 'King Sub' coming after despite its lower number.
  - Rolex Passion Market and Rolex Magazine suggest the 6200 was likely the first physically produced but held back commercially in favour of the slimmer 6204 launched at Basel 1954.
- **Whether the 5512/5513 are 'tool watches' or have become luxury items** — Not a factual conflict so much as a tonal one — useful for the demand-story / cultural-context section.  ·  _scope: 5513, modern, model-wide_
  - Teddy Baldassarre and Hodinkee frame the 5513 as Rolex's quintessential approachable tool watch, especially in matte-dial form.
  - Fratello observes the modern Submariner has shifted toward luxury/'wrist-flex' territory, and Bob's Watches notes the 1980s glossy/white-gold-surround dials as the first hint of luxury creep into the 5513 itself.

## Stories worth telling (10)

- **Jeanneret, Cousteau and the birth of the Submariner** — The founding human story — a Rolex director and diver, friends with Cousteau, persuades the board to build a sporty waterproof watch. Anchors the model-wide origin section.  
  [source](https://monochrome-watches.com/rolex-submariner-history-part-1-the-early-references/)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **Piccard's Bathyscaphe and the 1953 Deep Sea Special** — The marketing stunt that announced Rolex's deep-water credentials: a specially built Rolex strapped to the outside of Piccard's bathyscaphe descending 3,131.8m, returning unscathed, just as the consumer Submariner was about to be launched.  
  [source](https://monochrome-watches.com/rolex-submariner-history-part-1-the-early-references/)  ·  _scope: model-wide_
- **Connery's 6538 and the original Bond watch** — The single most important pop-culture moment for the early Submariner; recurs across nearly every source as the watch that made the Sub culturally inescapable.  
  [source](https://www.gq.com/story/rolex-submariner-buying-guide)  ·  _scope: 6538, bond, early_
- **The Roger Moore 'Q' 5513 — Live and Let Die** — A genuinely distinctive 5513-specific Bond story: a screen-used 5513 modified by Syd Cain with a fictional buzz-saw bezel and magnetic field, caseback engraved 'Roger Moore 007', sold by Phillips.  
  [source](https://www.phillips.com/detail/rolex/CH080515/290)  ·  _scope: 5513, bond_
- **'The Shadow' — South African Special Forces 5513** — The only known factory-blacked-out Rolex: a 1975 5513 in gunmetal teflon coating, made for South African special forces during Operation Savannah, later confirmed by Rolex Patrimoine. Inspired the entire blacked-out custom Sub genre (Bamford, Pro Hunter).  
  [source](https://perezcope.com/2023/05/07/the-shadow-a-unique-factory-blacked-out-rolex-submariner-5513-milsub/)  ·  _scope: 5513, milsub_
- **Stuart L.'s 1971 COMEX 5513 prototype** — A documented civilian-issued 5513 (2.8m serial, 1971) retrofitted with a prototype HEV and used in commercial deep diving — provenance via a letter from the original Royal Navy / COMEX diver. Concrete evidence that some 5513s left the factory with valves before the dedicated 5514.  
  [source](https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-blog/history-of-time/vintage-rolex-submariner-5513-comex-prototype.html)  ·  _scope: 5513, comex_
- **Steve McQueen's 5512/5513** — Often misremembered as an Explorer II story, McQueen actually wore a Submariner — Wind Vintage and GQ both note the 5512 as his Sub of choice, which underpins much of the watch's mid-century cool-factor.  
  [source](https://www.windvintage.com/blog/collectors-guide-rolex-submariner-reference-5512)  ·  _scope: 5512_
- **Teddy's father's 1982 Feet-First 5513 and grandfather's 1967 Meters-First 5513** — A real-life illustration of how Rolex servicing in the early 2000s could transform a Meters-First matte 5513 into something resembling a late 1989 model (Luminova, white-gold surrounds, no tritium) — a cautionary tale about originality and dial integrity.  
  [source](https://teddybaldassarre.com/blogs/watches/rolex-5513-submariner-review)  ·  _scope: 5513_
- **Cousteau testing Submariner prototypes in 1953** — Photographic evidence cited by Rolex Magazine ties the model's earliest prototypes to Cousteau personally, reinforcing the Jeanneret/Cousteau founding myth.  
  [source](https://www.rolexmagazine.com/2020/08/a-brief-history-of-rolex-submariner.html)  ·  _scope: model-wide, early_
- **Chuck Yeager and Elvis in Big-Crown Subs** — Brief but evocative cultural anchors for the pre-5512 era — Yeager (1956) and Elvis (1962) photographed wearing 6538s.  
  [source](https://www.rolexmagazine.com/2020/08/a-brief-history-of-rolex-submariner.html)  ·  _scope: 6538, early_

## Module candidates — future nodes (8)

- **James Bond and the Submariner** — Two distinct Bond stories — Connery's 6538 in Dr. No / FRWL / Goldfinger, and Moore's 5513 in Live and Let Die etc., plus the Syd Cain 'Q' 5513 prop — deserve their own page with auction provenance from Phillips, Christie's and Bonhams.
- **COMEX Submariners (5513 GEV, 5514, COMEX-dialled 1680)** — The corpus has rich, primary-source material on the Rolex–COMEX partnership from 1970/71, the prototype 5513 with HEV, and the dedicated 5514. Deserves a standalone page so it doesn't crowd a 5512/5513 reference page.
- **MilSub (5513 & 5517)** — Distinct British MoD-issue feature set (fixed bars, sword hands, T-circle, fully graduated bezel) plus the 'Shadow' South African outlier and the Licence to Kill prop — a clear dedicated topic.
- **Sea-Dweller and the Gas Escape Valve** — The 1665 / Single Red / Double Red / 16660 / Doxa controversy is a complete narrative arc that overlaps with but is distinct from the Submariner — and Perezcope's deep research deserves a home that won't muddy the Sub page.
- **Gold and precious-metal Submariners (1680/8, 16808, 16618, 116618, 116619 'Smurf')** — VRFM provides a deep, self-contained collector's guide including dial Marks, weights, gold value and alloy evolution — a natural standalone page.
- **Red Sub (1680) dials** — VRFM's MkI–MkVII taxonomy of Red Sub dials is granular enough to warrant its own page.
- **Rolex Submariner bracelets** — The rivet → folded-link 9315 → solid-link 93150 evolution, plus Gay Frères/C&I suppliers, is a transversal topic that applies to multiple Sub references.
- **Maxi-dial 5513 (Beaumont Miller II's taxonomy)** — The 5513mattedial.com material on Maxi I–V is highly specialised and could be a dedicated sub-page off the 5513.
