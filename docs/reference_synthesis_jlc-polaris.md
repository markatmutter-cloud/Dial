## Model context

The Jaeger-LeCoultre Polaris sits in JLC's catalogue as the maison's most recognisable sports/dive line, anchored by the vintage Memovox Polaris ref. E859 of the late 1960s and revived as a full collection in 2018. The line traces directly from the Memovox alarm wristwatch of 1950, through the Memovox Deep Sea (E857) of 1959 — the first automatic dive watch with an alarm — to the E859 prototypes of 1963 and the production E859 from 1965. The Polaris name itself originated with JLC's US distributor, evoking Cold War-era polar/space/undersea adventure (the Polaris submarine-launched missile is widely cited as the inspiration). The vintage E859 was an answer to the dive-watch boom of the 1950s on JLC's own terms: rather than copy the external rotating bezel of the Submariner or Fifty Fathoms, JLC used an audible alarm as a dive-time warning, paired with an internal rotating bezel, a Piquerez (EPSA) Compressor case, and a multi-layer caseback engineered to make the alarm audible underwater. Total vintage production was tiny — 1,714 pieces across 1965–1970 — which combined with the boldly legible 42mm dial, three crowns and oversized Hesalite crystal to make it a cult collector watch by the 2000s. JLC tested the demand with the 2008 Memovox Tribute to Polaris (ref. 190.8.96, 768 pieces), then in 2018 used the 50th anniversary of the 1968 dial as the launch point for a full modern Polaris range — three-hand, date, chronograph, world-time chronograph and Memovox LE — repositioning Polaris as JLC's everyday sport-elegance line alongside the dressier Reverso and Master collections. The line has since expanded to include Mariner (true 300m dive) models, gradient-dial editions, a Geographic and a Perpetual Calendar, treating the Polaris case as a platform for JLC's broader watchmaking rather than only an alarm diver.

  - Independent scholarship (Mstanga via Le Monde Edmond, Hairspring, Fratello, Christie's) documents 50 prototypes in 1963 and series production from 1965, with 1968 simply being the largest production year and the year of the bolder updated dial.
- **Original case diameter — 41mm or 42mm?** — Consensus is 42mm; treat 41mm as a Hairspring measurement variance rather than a separate case size.  ·  _scope: E859_
  - Christie's, JLC, Le Monde Edmond, Fratello, S.Song Watches and Watchonista all cite 42mm.
  - Hairspring's two E859 features cite 41mm.
- **Total production count** — Use 1,714 production examples + ~50 separate prototypes as the canonical figure.  ·  _scope: E859_
  - Most sources cite 1,714 pieces total across all variants (1965–1970), excluding the 50 prototypes (Le Monde Edmond, Christie's, Hairspring, S.Song, Watchesbysjx, Hodinkee).
  - Fratello phrases it as 1,714 'units of the Memovox Polaris' including both generations, but does not exclude prototypes explicitly.
- **Was the Polaris named after the missile, the star or the polar/space exploration era?** — Both framings can coexist — the US arm wanted exploration-era resonance, and the Polaris missile name was part of that cultural backdrop.  ·  _scope: E859, model-wide_
  - Fratello attributes it directly to the Cold War Polaris submarine-launched ballistic missile, which was prominent in 1960s US culture.
  - Le Monde Edmond's interviewee Mstanga frames it more broadly as the US division wanting a name to convey the spirit of polar, oceanic and space adventure.
- **How many generations of vintage Polaris dial exist?** — The 'two generations + Calypso' framing is the most useful collector shorthand; the 1967 examples are transitional.  ·  _scope: E859_
  - Fratello argues for two clear generations (1965 first-gen with small plots, 1966+ second-gen with trapezoidal plots and matte dial).
  - Christie's frames the 1967 production as a small test batch for the new bolder dial that defined 1968.
  - Le Monde Edmond enumerates the model years (1965, 1966, 1967, 1968, plus 1970 Calypso) without sharply dividing into two generations.
- **Polaris II (E870) production years** — Treat as roughly 1970–1971/72; if precision is needed, Fratello's archival sourcing is the most reliable.  ·  _scope: E870_
  - Fratello: produced 1970 and 1971.
  - Swisswatches Magazine: introduced 1970 as successor.
  - Watchonista: 1970–1972.

## Stories worth telling (9)

- **The American who asked for a dive alarm** — Bertram S. Lowe, president of LeCoultre Watch Inc. in New York, wrote to the JLC board in 1959 proposing a dive watch with an alarm — and again in 1961 specifically suggesting a Piquerez compressor case. The Polaris exists because of an American distributor's idea.  
  [source](https://le-monde-edmond.com/polaris-e859-jaeger-indepthreview/)  ·  _scope: E859, model-wide_
- **The 1967 'test dial' batch of nine** — A documented sub-run of just nine pieces produced in 1967 carries '67' inside the caseback and is thought to have been a small trial of the bolder 1968 dial — exceptional rarity inside an already rare reference.  
  [source](https://hairspring.com/blogs/finds/e859-jaeger-lecoultre-polaris)  ·  _scope: E859_
- **The Calypso-hands oddity** — In 1970 JLC produced approximately 55 E859s with an unusual 'Calypso' hand set; collectors regard them as a controversial footnote to the run — small enough in number to be a curiosity rather than a desired variant.  
  [source](https://le-monde-edmond.com/polaris-e859-jaeger-indepthreview/)  ·  _scope: E859_
- **The dial that became an aquarium** — The bezel-less construction under an oversized domed Hesalite crystal makes the matte black gen-2 dial appear to float beneath a pool of glass — a much-repeated descriptor that captures the watch's visual signature.  
  [source](https://hairspring.com/blogs/finds/e859-jaeger-lecoultre-polaris)  ·  _scope: E859_
- **Why the modern Polaris US dial side was never re-issued** — The 2008 Memovox Tribute to Polaris faithfully copied the European 'Jaeger-LeCoultre' dial; the US-market 'LeCoultre'-signed configuration has never been re-issued, making originals quietly distinctive among collectors.  
  [source](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/just-because-a-1968-lecoultre-polaris)  ·  _scope: E859_
- **Benedict Cumberbatch and 'In a Breath'** — As a JLC ambassador, Benedict Cumberbatch wore the Polaris Mariner Memovox for the short film 'In a Breath' — a notable modern wrist for the line.  
  [source](https://thehourmarkers.com/articles/retracing-the-journey-of-the-jaegerlecoultre-polaris)  ·  _scope: Q9028471_
- **The 'Lucky 13' one-off** — In 2019, JLC made a one-off Polaris Date (cal. 899AA) with the number 13 engraved on the rotor to commemorate the 13 complications of the Hybris Mechanica à Grande Sonnerie; auctioned that December.  
  [source](https://thehourmarkers.com/articles/retracing-the-journey-of-the-jaegerlecoultre-polaris)  ·  _scope: Q9068670_
- **The 768-piece bridge** — The 2008 Tribute to Polaris (190.8.96) was the watch that proved there was real modern demand for the design — its 768-piece run sold through and set the stage for the 2018 collection.  
  [source](https://hairspring.com/blogs/finds/1-of-768-jaeger-lecoultre-polaris-limited-190-8-96)  ·  _scope: 190.8.96_
- **Putting the alarm where you can see it** — For the 2020 Mariner Memovox, JLC re-engineered the cal. 956 so the gong attaches to the side of the case rather than the caseback — allowing a sapphire window onto the hammer in motion while preserving 300m water resistance.  
  [source](https://www.fratellowatches.com/hands-on-jaeger-lecoultre-polaris-mariner-memovox-review/)  ·  _scope: Q9028471_
