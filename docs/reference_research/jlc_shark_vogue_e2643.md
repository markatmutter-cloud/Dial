# Jaeger-LeCoultre / LeCoultre Shark Deep Sea / Vogue Chronograph — Ref. E2643

_Source-derived reference note for vintage watch research / Watchlist reference page_

---

## 1. One-paragraph reference summary

The Jaeger-LeCoultre / LeCoultre **ref. E2643** is a late-1960s manually wound steel chronograph known as the **“Shark Deep Sea”** in the U.S. market and the **“Vogue Chronograph”** in Europe. It is most often seen with a black reverse-panda dial, tachymeter scale, large angular steel case, rotating bezel, and a Valjoux-derived manual chronograph movement. Its defining feature is the use of interchangeable bezels — typically described as **diving / 60-minute**, **telemeter**, and **world-time** bezels — giving the watch a hybrid identity across diver, travel watch, and sports chronograph.

Key sources: [Jaeger-LeCoultre Collectibles — Deep Sea Shark QVE26431](https://www.jaeger-lecoultre.com/us-en/watches/collectibles/qve26431); [Phillips — Geneva Watch Auction FIVE, Lot 97](https://www.phillips.com/detail/JAEGER-LECOULTRE/CH080117/97); [Phillips — Hong Kong Watch Auction XII, Lot 931](https://www.phillips.com/detail/jaegerlecoultre/150931)

## 3. Model identity and naming

The E2643 has a split identity that is important for collecting and cataloguing. Phillips repeatedly states that the watch was sold as the **“Shark Deep Sea”** in the U.S. market and as the **“Vogue Chronograph”** in Europe. This is one of the clearest source-backed facts about the model.

Phillips also notes that **LeCoultre-signed dials** generally indicate watches made for the American market, while Jaeger-LeCoultre signatures are associated with non-U.S. or European-market examples. This should be used as a strong clue, not as a complete authentication rule on its own.

## 4. Why the watch matters

The E2643 is interesting because it does not fit neatly into one category. It is a chronograph, a dive-adjacent tool watch, and — when fitted with the world-time bezel — a travel watch. Jaeger-LeCoultre’s own Collectibles listing describes the model around its ability to change function through bezel swaps, with bezels for diving, telemeter use, and world-time reading.

This makes it a useful “bridge watch” for collector mapping. It connects:

- JLC diving watches such as the Deep Sea Alarm, Polaris, and other 1960s sport references
- Valjoux 72 manual-wind chronographs such as the Daytona, Autavia, and Navitimer ecosystem
- 1960s multi-function travel/tool watches with world-time or 24-hour bezels
- Oversized late-1960s sports chronographs with angular cases and experimental identities

## 5. Case and design notes

The E2643 case is one of the strongest parts of the watch. Auction houses and collector sources consistently describe it as large for the period, with broad, angular lugs and a strong sporty profile. Phillips Geneva Watch Auction FOUR describes the case as 40mm and notes the broad/angular lugs and modern styling. JLC’s Collectibles listing gives the modern Collectibles example as 40mm in steel with 13mm thickness.

Design characteristics to track in listings:

- Thick, angular lugs
- Large steel case, usually around 40mm
- Prominent crown and pump pushers
- Acrylic crystal
- Rotating bezel, often world-time or telemeter in auction photos
- Reverse-panda dial with strong legibility
- Inner tachymeter scale
- Tritium hour markers and hands

## 6. Dial variants

### 6.1 Reverse-panda dial

The standard and most recognizable configuration is a black dial with light subdials. JLC’s Collectibles page describes a reverse-panda black grained dial with luminescent hour markers and stick hands. Phillips Hong Kong XII and TOKI also show reverse-panda examples.

## 7. Bezel system and variants

The bezel system is the defining technical/collector feature of the E2643. JLC and Phillips describe the model as having multiple interchangeable bezels. These are generally:

1. **60-minute / diving bezel** — turns the watch toward casual/dive timing use.
2. **Telemeter bezel** — supports distance measurement, typically linked to timing an event that is both seen and heard.
3. **World-time / 24-hour bezel** — turns the watch into a travel/world-time companion.

Sources:

- [JLC Collectibles — Deep Sea Shark QVE26431](https://www.jaeger-lecoultre.com/us-en/watches/collectibles/qve26431)

### 7.1 World-time bezel

The world-time bezel is often the most visually distinctive and the one most likely to make the watch feel like a travel chronograph rather than a dive chronograph. Phillips Start-Stop-Reset describes a black-dial E2643 with world-time revolving bezel.

Source:

- [Phillips — START-STOP-RESET, Lot 88](https://phillips.com/detail/jaegerlecoultre/97920)

### 7.2 White 24-hour bezel / “Great White Shark”

Phillips Hong Kong Watch Auction X describes a rare example nicknamed the **“Great White Shark”** because of its unusual white aluminium 24-hour bezel. Phillips suggests this may have been a possible American bezel prototype and notes that the example had a LeCoultre dial signature and VXN import code on the movement.

Source:

Suggested page note:

> The white 24-hour bezel variant should be treated as a specialist sub-variant, not a normal production configuration. Phillips describes it as rare and possibly linked to U.S.-market examples.

## 8. Movement notes

The E2643 is powered by manually wound Valjoux-derived chronograph calibres. Many examples are listed with **Valjoux cal. 72 / JLC cal. 72**, while some later examples are listed with **cal. 726**.

Important source examples:

- Phillips Hong Kong XII: 1969 E2643, manual **cal. 72**, 17 jewels.
- Phillips Hong Kong X “Great White Shark”: circa 1969, manual **cal. 72**, 17 jewels.
- Phillips Start-Stop-Reset: circa 1969, manual **cal. 726**, 17 jewels.
- Phillips TOKI: circa 1971, manual **cal. 726**, 17 jewels; Phillips describes cal. 726 as an upgraded 21,600 a/h successor to the Valjoux 72.

## 12. Suggested Watchlist page intro

> The Jaeger-LeCoultre E2643 is one of the stranger and more compelling sports chronographs of the late 1960s. Sold as the **Shark Deep Sea** in the U.S. and the **Vogue Chronograph** in Europe, it combined a large steel case, manually wound Valjoux chronograph movement, dive-watch stance, tachymeter scale, and a system of interchangeable bezels for diving, telemeter, and world-time use. Most examples are reverse-panda, with LeCoultre-signed dials generally pointing to U.S.-market delivery and Jaeger-LeCoultre signatures to European-market watches. It is not as obvious as a Speedmaster, Autavia, Daytona, or Seamaster 300, but that is the point: the E2643 is a technical, slightly eccentric, highly period-specific JLC for collectors who like tool watches with more than one identity.

## 14. Practical buying notes

For a potential purchase, prioritize:

1. Clear dial photos, including signature, subdial printing, lume plots, and tachymeter scale.
2. Confirmed movement calibre and movement photos.
3. Caseback interior/exterior photos and reference engraving.
4. Bezel inventory: fitted bezel plus any additional bezels.
5. Original box / bezel holder / guarantee / archive extract where available.
6. Case condition, especially lug profile and signs of polishing.
7. Whether the seller is describing the watch as Shark, Vogue, Deep Sea, or generic JLC chronograph — terminology varies, but the reference should be clear.

## 16. Source hierarchy

For future edits, use this hierarchy:

1. **Jaeger-LeCoultre official Collectibles / archive material** — highest confidence for brand-stated specs and intended functionality.
2. **Phillips lot essays and cataloguing** — strongest auction-market and variant documentation.
3. **Other major auction houses** — useful for additional sale records and images.
4. **Specialist dealers** — useful for photos, condition language, and market framing, but verify against primary examples.
5. **Collector forums** — useful for catalog scans, community memory, and warnings; do not treat as definitive without corroboration.
6. **Marketplace aggregators** — useful for current listings and price context, but weak for historical facts.
